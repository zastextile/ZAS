WHAT YOUR SCREENSHOT SHOWS, AND WHY
------------------------------------
Look at the form in your screenshot:

    Product Name      [Bed shee…]        <- right
    Description       [Plain dyed]       <- right
    Category          [Default Un…]      <- WRONG
    40ft HC Capacity  [Last Sale]        <- WRONG

"Default Unit" and "Last Sale" are LABELS. They are sitting where their
neighbour's INPUT BOX should be. The form is telling you Category is called
Default Unit, which is nonsense, and the two real fields are squeezed to
slivers you cannot see.

THE CAUSE IS MINE, AND IT IS SIMPLE. To keep the form short I put two fields
on one row:

    [ field ] [ label ] [ field ]        (a 3-column grid)

That works at a comfortable width. But this form sits in HALF OF A SPLIT —
Size Variants has the other half — so those three cells were sharing about
200 pixels. The middle label was set not to wrap, so it took its full width
first and both fields collapsed to almost nothing. What you are left with on
screen is a label standing where a box should be.

A LABEL POINTING AT THE WRONG BOX IS WORSE THAN A FORM ONE ROW TALLER. So the
pairing is gone, not patched:

    Product Name      [ … ]
    Description       [ … ]
    Category          [ … ]
    Default Unit      [ … ]
    40ft HC Capacity  [ … ]
    Last Sale         [ … ]
    Active            [x] in use for new orders

One field per row. Every label points at the box beside it. Seven short rows
— still far shorter than the two big cards this replaced, and now it cannot
be misread at any width.

I ALSO REMOVED THE RULE ITSELF from app.css, not just its use, with a note
saying why. If two fields ever share a row again they need a container query
that knows how wide the PANE is — a fixed 3-column grid cannot know that, and
will fail the same way.

AND THE LABEL COLUMN NOW GIVES WAY. It was a hard 130px, which in a narrow
pane leaves very little for the field. It is now min(130px, 38%) — 130px when
there is room, proportional when there is not.


WHY YOU MUST UPLOAD includes/layout.php TOO
--------------------------------------------
It carries ?v=29 on the stylesheet. Without it your browser keeps the CSS it
already has, and a form built for new rules gets old ones — which is the
exact fault that made the whole page look unstyled two releases ago. The CSS
and the pages that use it move together. Always.


THE OTHER THING IN YOUR SCREENSHOT
-----------------------------------
"Bed sheet — 0 parts · 4 size variants · ready for production"

ZERO PARTS. Every size variant reads "No parts yet". That product cannot be
produced, whatever the badge says — and this is the same gate 4 the v29
diagnostic reports on the production screens. The badge says "ready for
production" because the checklist counts a whole-set operation (your CUTTING
and HEM rows have a blank Part, which means "the whole set") as enough.

I have NOT changed that badge in this zip, because it is a judgement call and
I would rather ask than guess: a bed sheet genuinely may be a single-part
product with no named parts at all, in which case "ready" is correct and your
CUTTING and HEM rows are all it needs. But if you expect every product to
carry named parts imported from the Part Library, then "0 parts" should read
as NOT ready, and I will change the checklist to say so.
